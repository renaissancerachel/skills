# Simple summary

Summarize the current work in SIMPLE, plain terms. Assume the reader is smart but busy and
non-technical about the details. Give *just enough* context to make an educated decision —
not a data dump, not everything you know. Optimize for: readable in under a minute, and they
know exactly what to say back.

If the user names a specific thing to summarize, scope to that. Otherwise summarize everything
relevant to where the work stands right now.

## Rules for the summary
- **Plain language.** No jargon. If a technical thing matters, explain it in one short everyday
  sentence ("OneDrive looks like a backup but isn't").
- **Tie every item to a plain reason.** Never list a fact without saying why it matters to them.
- **Short.** Trim anything they don't need to decide. Favor a few clear sentences over completeness.
- **Be honest about state.** If nothing has actually happened yet (just plans), say so. If
  something failed or a step was skipped, say that plainly — don't round up to "done."
- **No action happens without a yes.** This is reporting and asking, not doing. Do not start
  side-effectful work from this summary.
- **Warm and calm in tone.** A little human, not a robot status dump.

## Shape (adapt headers to fit — skip any that don't apply)

**What I did / where things stand**
- Bullet points, plain terms. What's actually true right now.

**What it means / what I found** (use numbered points when there are findings or options)
1. The finding in one line. → Then a short plan or implication. ("→ Plan: copy them somewhere
   safe, one at a time, checking each.")

**One thing to know** (only if there's a real caveat, risk, glitch, or gotcha)
- The thing, stated calmly, and whether it was left alone or handled.

**What I need from you** (the decisions, as a short numbered list)
1. A clear either/or question, with a recommendation if there is one.
2. ...keep it to the few calls that actually unblock the next step.

End with one short reassuring line that nothing happens until they answer.
